## Background

The product team was exploring how multiple applications could merge into a new experience for customers managing events that affect their business.
The initial ask was essentially:
Multiple applications are coming together. What should the new experience be?
The research challenge was to first get clear on the underlying customer problem rather than prematurely designing the solution.

## Research opportunity

Personas included Azure customers across industries, including Finance, Healthcare, and Entertainment:

The research team hypothesized that customers operating their businesses on Azure follow a similar decision-making model when responding to events that impact their business.

## Research Program

This was a **series of studies designed to progressively build knowledge about the customer and the scenario.**

### Scenarios

- Troubleshooting
- Outages
- Service retirements
- Proactive resilience

### Research Program

**Persona → Scenario → Decision-making → Information needs → Experience opportunities**

---

## Research Question

The research evolved from a product-focused question to a customer-centered research question.

### Initial Product Question

> What should the new merged experience look like?

↓

### Better Research Question

> How do IT administrators make decisions when cloud events affect their business, and what information do they need at each point in that journey?

This distinction was important because it shifted the focus from **designing a new experience** to understanding the **underlying customer decision-making process**.

---

## Hypothesis

Customers operating their business on Azure follow a similar decision model when responding to business-impacting events.

The research supported this hypothesis.

However, an important nuance emerged:

> **Criticality and business impact determine urgency.**

This became a major insight from the research.

The experience therefore shouldn't simply respond to the **type of event**. It needs to understand the customer's **situation, impact, and urgency**.

---

## Research Synthesis

The research produced a conceptual decision framework describing how customers navigate business-impacting cloud events:

**Event occurs**  
↓  
**Assess impact**  
↓  
**Determine criticality**  
↓  
**Understand what happened**  
↓  
**Determine what action is needed**  
↓  
**Take action**  
↓  
**Monitor / recover / prevent recurrence**

---

## Research Outputs

The research produced several important product outputs.

### 1. Information Needs Framework

Developed a framework describing the information customers need throughout the event-management journey.

**Outcome:** Created a reusable framework that helped product teams understand what information customers need at different points in the decision-making process.

---

### 2. Communication Evaluation Rubric

Created a rubric for evaluating the quality of customer communications.

The rubric helped product teams determine whether communications were sufficiently:

- **Relevant**
- **Clear**
- **Actionable**
- **Timely**
- **Appropriate to the customer's situation**

---

### Telemetry & Behavioral Data

Used telemetry volume, including retirement-related support-ticket volume, to understand the scale and nature of customer problems.

This allowed qualitative research and behavioral data to complement one another:

Customer insight + Behavioral evidence → Stronger understanding of the problem

### Agentic Workflow Opportunities

The research identified opportunities to trigger agentic workflows at specific points in the customer journey.

The point was not simply that "AI could help."

The research identified where AI and agents could meaningfully intervene in the customer journey based on observed needs and decision points.

### Research MCP Server

Research outputs were structured and made available through a research MCP server so product teams could embed research knowledge into AI workflows.

Research was transformed from a static deliverable into reusable knowledge that could be accessed by product and AI workflows.

This created a path for research findings to become part of ongoing product and AI decision-making rather than remaining a one-time research artifact.

### Executive Communication

Research findings were presented to VP-level stakeholders, translating customer evidence into frameworks and product strategy.
